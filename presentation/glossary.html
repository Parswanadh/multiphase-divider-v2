analogLib

Cadence/EDA

Cadence standard primitive component library containing ideal electrical elements such as independent sources (vpulse, vdc), ground symbols (gnd!), and ideal passive resistors (res) and capacitors (cap).

In this project: Used in `divider_lib` schematics for supply sources (`vdc`), differential clock stimuli (`vpulse`), and ideal load resistors (`res`). The ideal 2-terminal `res` later created an LVS mismatch against the 3-terminal PDK resistor `g45rnsnp`.

Antenna Effect

Layout

Plasma-induced gate oxide damage occurring during reactive ion etching when long, unconnected metal interconnect lines accumulate charge that discharges through thin transistor gate oxides.

In this project: Monitored during Assura DRC checks; in our placement layout, transistor gates are kept at unit lengths and connected to landing pads to prevent oxide breakdown during future routing passes.
→ View in Section 6: Layout & Physical Verification #term-antenna-effect

Aspect Ratio (W/L)

Sizing

The ratio of channel width to channel length in a MOSFET, governing current drive capacity ($I_{ds} \propto W/L$), transconductance ($g_m$), and parasitic gate capacitance.

In this project: Unit NMOS devices (M1–M6) use $W/L = 240\text{ nm} / 45\text{ nm} = 5.33$, closely matching Kuo et al.'s reference ratio of $5.00$ ($900\text{ nm} / 180\text{ nm}$). Tail device M7 uses $480\text{ nm} / 90\text{ nm} = 5.33$.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-aspect-ratio--w-l

Assura

Verification

Cadence physical verification suite performing Design Rule Checking (DRC), Layout Versus Schematic (LVS), and Parasitic Extraction (RCX) on IC layouts.

In this project: Assura 4.1 was run in batch mode inside the CentOS VM via `run_verify.sh` to check `divider_lib/DLATCH` and `divider_core` layouts against `assuraDRC.rul` and `extract.rul`.
→ View in Section 7: Cadence Tool Tour #term-assura

B

Bias Voltage (vb)

Circuit

A steady DC control potential applied to the gate of an MOS transistor to set its quiescent gate-to-source bias ($V_{gs}$) and determine its operating current.

In this project: Nominal tail bias $v_b = 1.0\text{ V}$ is applied to M7's gate. Swept between $0.8\text{ V}$ and $1.0\text{ V}$ during initial sizing exploration; $1.0\text{ V}$ was selected to maximize pull-down drive headroom.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-bias-voltage--vb

Body Effect (Substrate Bias)

Device physics

The shift in a MOSFET's threshold voltage ($V_{th}$) caused by a non-zero voltage difference between source and substrate ($V_{sb} > 0$), widening the depletion region.

In this project: In the stacked NMOS sampling network (M1 above M5 above M7), sources of M1 and M5 sit above ground ($V_s > 0$), raising their effective $V_{th}$ and increasing stack on-resistance.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-body-effect--substrate-bias

CDF (Component Description Format)

Cadence/EDA

Cadence proprietary data structure that defines component parameters, simulation models, terminal labels, and callback procedures for Virtuoso library cells.

In this project: Interacted with via SKILL (`cdfGetInstCDF`) in `build_schematic_v2.il` to set device width (`w`), length (`l`), multiplier (`m`), and finger counts before schematic netlisting.

Channel Length Modulation (lambda)

Device physics

The shortening of the effective inverted channel length in a MOSFET as drain-to-source voltage ($V_{ds}$) increases beyond pinch-off, causing a finite output conductance $g_{ds}$.

In this project: Accounted for by the BSIM4 model in GPDK045; tail transistor M7 uses a double-length channel ($L = 90\text{ nm}$) to mitigate channel length modulation and improve output resistance.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-channel-length-modulation--lambda

Channel On-Resistance (Ron)

Sizing

The effective electrical resistance between drain and source terminals of an MOS transistor operating in the deep linear (triode) conduction region: $R_{on} \approx [\mu C_{ox} (W/L) (V_{gs} - V_{th})]^{-1}$.

In this project: Measured via DC sweep in GPDK045 for unit NMOS ($240\text{ nm} / 45\text{ nm}$) at $V_{gs} = 1.0\text{ V}, V_{ds} = 10\text{--}100\text{ mV}$ to be $R_{on} = 2.3\text{ k}\Omega$ ($2.28\text{--}2.73\text{ k}\Omega$).
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-channel-on-resistance--ron

In this project: Invoked in batch mode via `cadence/skill/auto/route_dlatch.il` on `DLATCH/layout_lxl`, routing 136 of 137 connections (99.27% completion) with 1 unrouted net (`xs`).
→ View in Section 6: Layout & Physical Verification #term-chip-assembly-router--vcar

CIW (Command Interpreter Window)

Cadence/EDA

The primary control and console logging window of Cadence Virtuoso where user commands, SKILL expressions, load logs, and tool error messages are output.

In this project: Captured in `virtuoso_ciw_gpdk045_loaded.png` to confirm proper initialization of `gpdk045_v_6_0` and automated execution of batch SKILL scripts without interactive intervention.
→ View in Section 7: Cadence Tool Tour #term-ciw--command-interpreter-window

CK / CKb

Circuit

Complementary differential clock signals where CKb is 180° out of phase with CK, providing non-overlapping control for sampling and regeneration phases.

In this project: Input differential clocks run at 1.4 GHz (Design D) and 6.0 GHz (Design B). Inverted clock connections (CKb to sampling, CK to hold) are applied to latches 2 and 3 in the ring.
→ View in Section 1: The Paper (Kuo et al.) #term-ck---ckb

CMOS (Complementary MOS)

Device physics

Integrated circuit technology combining both n-type (NMOS) and p-type (PMOS) field-effect transistors on a single silicon substrate to achieve high noise immunity and low static power.

In this project: The core ring divider employs an NMOS current-steering topology with resistive loads to maximize switching speed and eliminate PMOS gate-loading capacitance.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-cmos--complementary-mos

Cross-Coupled Pair

Circuit

Two active transistors where the drain of each is cross-connected to the gate of the other, forming a regenerative positive feedback loop with high differential negative resistance.

In this project: Transistors M3 and M4 in each DLATCH form the cross-coupled regeneration pair, holding and regenerating the sampled state when clock switch M6 is enabled by CKb.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-cross-coupled-pair

Project-specific

In this project: Configured with device multiplier $m = 4$, load resistors $R_1 = R_2 = 2\text{ k}\Omega$, achieving 3000.0 MHz output, $0.493\text{ mW}$ power, $27.94\text{ ps}$ delay, $13.77\text{ fJ}$ PDP, and $3.78^\circ$ phase error.
→ View in Section 4: Results Dashboard #term-design-b--6-0-ghz

Design D (1.4 GHz)

Project-specific

The nominal, PVT-robust sized frequency divider variant designed for operation at the reference paper's input clock frequency of 1.4 GHz.

In this project: Configured with multiplier $m = 4$, load resistors $R_1 = R_2 = 3\text{ k}\Omega$, achieving 700.0 MHz output, $0.541\text{ mW}$ power, $25.99\text{ ps}$ delay, $14.07\text{ fJ}$ PDP, and 41/45 PVT corner yield.
→ View in Section 4: Results Dashboard #term-design-d--1-4-ghz

dfIIToVldb

Verification

Cadence executable that converts a Design Framework II (DFII) schematic view into a Volume Layout Database (VLDB) netlist file required by Assura LVS.

In this project: Called automatically within `cadence/skill/verify/run_verify.sh` using template `netlist.vlr.in` to translate `divider_lib/DLATCH` schematic into a format compatible with Assura.
→ View in Section 7: Cadence Tool Tour #term-dfiitovldb

Differential Pair

Circuit

A symmetrical two-transistor circuit topology that amplifies differential input voltage signals while rejecting common-mode noise and power supply fluctuations.

In this project: Transistors M1 and M2 in DLATCH form the input sampling differential pair, steering tail current between differential nodes Q and Qb based on inputs D and Db.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-differential-pair

Diffusion Area / Perimeter (as, ad, ps, pd)

Device physics

Geometric layout parameters specifying source/drain junction area (`as`, `ad`) and perimeter (`ps`, `pd`), directly determining junction parasitic capacitances.

In this project: Automatically calculated by Virtuoso PCell callbacks (`as=16.8f, ad=16.8f, ps=520n, pd=520n`) and injected into the netlist during schematic-driven simulations.
→ View in Section 5: Schematic vs Standalone #term-diffusion-area---perimeter--as--ad--ps--pd

Diffusion Series Resistance (nrd, nrs)

Device physics

Parameters representing the number of diffusion squares in series with drain (`nrd`) and source (`nrs`), determining extrinsic source/drain parasitic sheet resistance.

In this project: Extracted as `nrd=1.16667, nrs=1.16667` by the Virtuoso netlister; their addition to the standalone netlist contributed to explaining the $0.541\text{ mW} \to 0.455\text{ mW}$ power gap.
→ View in Section 5: Schematic vs Standalone #term-diffusion-series-resistance--nrd--nrs

Divide-by-Two

Circuit

A frequency division function where the fundamental frequency of the output signal is exactly half that of the input clock ($f_{out} = f_{in} / 2$).

In this project: Produces 700 MHz output from 1.4 GHz input (Design D) and 3000 MHz output from 6.0 GHz input (Design B) across all eight differential output phases.
→ View in Section 1: The Paper (Kuo et al.) #term-divide-by-two

DLATCH

Circuit

Differential D-type latch stage comprising a differential sampling pair, a regenerative cross-coupled pair, clock gating switches, resistive loads, and a tail current device.

In this project: The fundamental building block of the 8-phase divider; contains 7 NMOS transistors (M1–M7) and 2 load resistors (R1, R2). Four instances (XD1–XD4) form the closed ring.
→ View in Section 1: The Paper (Kuo et al.) #term-dlatch

DRC (Design Rule Checking)

Verification

Physical verification process confirming that a layout geometry complies with all semiconductor foundry manufacturing constraints (widths, spacings, overlaps, enclosures).

In this project: Executed using Assura 4.1 against `assuraDRC.rul`; DLATCH placement layout produced 21 real errors across 3 rules, while auto-routed layout produced 134 errors across 17 rules.
→ View in Section 6: Layout & Physical Verification #term-drc--design-rule-checking

Duty Cycle

Metrics

The ratio of high-logic duration to total waveform period ($T_{high} / T_{period} \times 100\%$).

In this project: Outputs exhibit balanced $50.0\% \pm 0.5\%$ duty cycle at steady state due to matched rise and fall delays ($t_{pdr} \approx t_{pdf} = 4RC$) and symmetrical differential topology.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-duty-cycle

E

Elmore Delay Model

Sizing

An analytical RC network delay approximation model that estimates signal propagation delay as the sum of resistances multiplied by the downstream downstream capacitances.

In this project: Applied to derive latch propagation delay: $t_{pdf} = (8C)(R/2) = 4RC$ and $t_{pdr} = (8C)(R_1) = 4RC$, demonstrating symmetric charging and discharging dynamics.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-elmore-delay-model

In this project: In commit `a805b27`, all hardcoded scripts and fabricated results were eliminated, establishing real Spectre MMSIM121 and Virtuoso IC615 verification pipelines in GPDK045.
→ View in Section 3: Project Timeline #term-fabrication-discovery--audit---recovery

Fan-Out (FO)

Sizing

The number of standard logic gate inputs driven by a single output node, determining effective capacitive loading and signal transition slew rates.

In this project: Each DLATCH output in the ring drives exactly one subsequent differential input pair ($FO = 1$) plus an assumed $10\text{ fF}$ interconnect and probing testbench capacitance.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-fan-out--fo

Femtojoule (fJ)

Metrics

A unit of electrical energy equal to $10^{-15}$ Joules ($1\text{ fJ} = 1\text{ mW} \times 1\text{ ps}$), commonly used to quantify power-delay product in nanoscale VLSI circuits.

In this project: Standard unit for PDP across all report tables: $14.067\text{ fJ}$ for Design D, $13.768\text{ fJ}$ for Design B, and $8.557\text{ fJ}$ for the Unsized Baseline.
→ View in Section 4: Results Dashboard #term-femtojoule--fj

Figure of Merit (FoM_phi)

Metrics

A composite performance metric in frequency divider literature balancing operating frequency, power dissipation, and phase accuracy: $\text{FoM}_\phi = 10 \log_{10}[f_{in} / (P \cdot \Delta\phi_{max})]$.

In this project: Calculated to compare 45 nm GPDK045 implementation efficiency against published 180 nm benchmark data from Kuo et al.
→ View in Section 4: Results Dashboard #term-figure-of-merit--fom-phi

File Lock (flock / vm.lock)

Project-specific

A POSIX file advisory locking mechanism used on the host operating system to serialize access to shared hardware resources.

In this project: Used via `flock /home/parshu/vm/vm.lock` across all host automation scripts (`vmx.sh`, `skill_run.sh`, `run_verify.sh`) to prevent concurrent execution conflicts on the VirtualBox guest.
→ View in Section 3: Project Timeline #term-file-lock--flock---vm-lock

Finger (nf)

Sizing

Dividing a wide transistor channel into multiple parallel narrower fingers sharing common source and drain diffusions to reduce gate resistance and diffusion capacitance.

In this project: Used in Layout XL generation where multiplier $m = 4$ devices were instantiated as 4 parallel fingers, resulting in 28 active MOS channels in view `layout_lxl`.

g45n1svt (nmos1v)

Device physics

The standard-threshold, 1.0 V nominal thin-oxide NMOS device provided in Cadence GPDK045, represented in Virtuoso schematics by cell `nmos1v`.

In this project: All active transistors (M1–M7) in `divider_lib` are instantiated using this model section with $W = 240\text{ nm}, L = 45\text{ nm}$ ($L = 90\text{ nm}$ for M7).

g45rnsnp

Project-specific

The non-silicided n-plus polysilicon resistor cell in Cadence GPDK045, featuring 3 electrical terminals (plus, minus, and substrate bulk tie).

gds (Output Conductance)

Device physics

The small-signal output conductance of a transistor ($g_{ds} = \partial I_{ds} / \partial V_{ds}$), representing the inverse of small-signal output channel resistance $r_o$.

In this project: Higher $g_{ds}$ in 45 nm short-channel devices lowers the intrinsic gain $g_m / g_{ds}$, necessitating higher load resistance values ($R_1$) to satisfy regeneration loop gain.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-gds--output-conductance

→ View in Section 6: Layout & Physical Verification #term-generate-from-source--lxgenfromsource

gm (Transconductance)

Device physics

The ratio of change in drain current to the change in gate-to-source voltage ($g_m = \partial I_{ds} / \partial V_{gs}$), measuring device amplification strength.

In this project: Regeneration requires loop gain $g_m R_1 > 1$ in the cross-coupled pair M3–M4. Sizing with multiplier $m = 4$ increases total $g_m$, enabling division with smaller load resistance.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-gm--transconductance

I

Inverter / Complementary Inversion

Circuit

Signal inversion logic function where logic high maps to low and vice versa.

In this project: In the differential ring divider, inversion is implemented without active inverters simply by cross-coupling complementary differential pairs ($Q_4 \to D_{1b}, Q_{4b} \to D_1$).

J

Jitter

Metrics

Undesired short-term variations and phase fluctuations of the significant clock transition edges from their ideal positions in time.

In this project: Minimized by symmetric differential switching and equalized rise/fall times; simulated waveforms exhibit sharp transitions with jitter $< 1.2\text{ ps}$.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-jitter

L

Latch-up

Layout

A destructive low-impedance short-circuit path formed between power and ground rails by parasitic bipolar transistors (p-n-p-n SCR structures) in bulk CMOS.

In this project: Governed by rule `LATCHUP.2` requiring substrate taps within maximum allowable distances from diffusion; router attempt triggered 56 `LATCHUP.2` violations due to tap spacing.
→ View in Section 6: Layout & Physical Verification #term-latch-up

LATCHUP.2 Rule

Layout

Assura DRC rule in GPDK045 mandating that NMOS source/drain diffusions must reside within a specified proximity to a p-substrate tie tap.

In this project: The single largest category of DRC violations (56 errors) produced during the automatic router attempt, requiring dedicated guard-ring and tap placement.
→ View in Section 6: Layout & Physical Verification #term-latchup-2-rule

Layout Versus Schematic (LVS)

Verification

Physical verification stage that compares extracted circuit connectivity from layout against the original schematic netlist to verify 1-to-1 equivalence.

In this project: Assura LVS run via `run_verify.sh`; reported device unbound errors due to 2-terminal schematic `analogLib/res` versus 3-terminal layout `g45rnsnp` resistors, plus open net `xs`.
→ View in Section 6: Layout & Physical Verification #term-layout-versus-schematic--lvs

Layout XL (Virtuoso Layout Suite XL)

Cadence/EDA

Connectivity-aware layout environment in Cadence Virtuoso that maintains real-time binding with the schematic, enabling guided placement, flying wires, and DRC checking.

In this project: Utilized in batch mode via `lxGenFromSource` (`cadence/skill/auto/lxl_flow.il`) to generate bound layout views with proper device terminals and nets.

Load Capacitance (C_L)

Sizing

The total lumped capacitive load present at an output node, consisting of gate oxide capacitances, junction diffusion capacitances, and interconnect parasitics.

In this project: Fixed at $10\text{ fF}$ per output terminal across all nominal and PVT characterization testbenches to model downstream loading and pad capacitance.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-load-capacitance--c-l

Load Resistor (R1, R2)

Circuit

Passive pull-up resistive elements connected between the positive supply rail (VDD) and the differential output nodes (Q, Qb).

In this project: Selected as $8\text{ k}\Omega$ for Unsized Baseline, $3\text{ k}\Omega$ for Design D, and $2\text{ k}\Omega$ for Design B; direct implementation of paper value ($1.15\text{ k}\Omega$) fails to divide.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-load-resistor--r1--r2

M

M1, M2 (Sampling Pair)

Circuit

Differential NMOS transistor pair responsible for sensing input signals D and Db during the clock sampling phase.

In this project: Configured with $W = 240\text{ nm}, L = 45\text{ nm}$, multiplier $m = 4$ in Design D. Source nodes connect to clock switch transistor M5 at intermediate node `xs`.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-m1--m2--sampling-pair

M3, M4 (Hold Cross-Coupled Pair)

Circuit

Cross-coupled NMOS transistor pair providing positive regenerative feedback to latch and store logic states during hold phase.

In this project: Configured identically to sampling devices with $W = 240\text{ nm}, L = 45\text{ nm}$, multiplier $m = 4$ in Design D. Sources connect to switch M6 at node `xh`.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-m3--m4--hold-cross-coupled-pair

M5 (Sampling Clock Switch)

Circuit

Clock-controlled switch transistor activated when CK is high to connect the sampling pair M1–M2 to the tail node.

In this project: Gate connected to clock signal CK; drain connects to sampling pair common source `xs`, source connects to tail node `tail`.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-m5--sampling-clock-switch

M6 (Hold Clock Switch)

Circuit

Clock-controlled switch transistor activated when complementary clock CKb is high to enable regenerative latching.

In this project: Gate connected to complementary clock CKb; drain connects to cross-coupled pair common source `xh`, source connects to tail node `tail`.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-m6--hold-clock-switch

M7 (Tail Current Source)

Circuit

NMOS transistor connected between common tail node and ground, biased at gate voltage vb to establish total bias current.

In this project: Sized with channel length $L = 90\text{ nm}$ and width $W = 480\text{ nm}$. Operates in triode region ($V_{ds} = 8\text{--}67\text{ mV} < V_{dsat} = 358\text{ mV}$) across all designs.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-m7--tail-current-source

Metal Layer / Routing Track (Metal1..Metal11)

Layout

Planar conductive aluminum/copper interconnect layers separated by dielectric insulating oxide in a CMOS process stack.

In this project: GPDK045 features 11 metal layers. Metal1 is used for internal cell pins and routing; Metal2 is used for inter-latch ring connections.
→ View in Section 6: Layout & Physical Verification #term-metal-layer---routing-track--metal1--metal11

METAL1.A.1 Rule

Layout

Assura DRC rule specifying that any polygon drawn on Metal1 must have a minimum planar surface area of at least $0.02\text{ }\mu\text{m}^2$.

In this project: Produced 14 real errors in the DLATCH placement layout where small square contact pin shapes fell below the minimum area constraint.
→ View in Section 6: Layout & Physical Verification #term-metal1-a-1-rule

Minimum Load Resistance (R_crit)

Sizing

The minimum load resistance required to establish a regenerative loop gain greater than unity ($g_m R_L > 1$) in a differential latch.

In this project: Derived as $R_{crit} = 1.1 \times (3R_{on} / m) = 7.59\text{ k}\Omega$ for $m=1$, and $1.90\text{ k}\Omega$ for $m=4$. Explains the failure of Kuo et al.'s $1.15\text{ k}\Omega$ rule.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-minimum-load-resistance--r-crit

Mobility (mu_n, mu_p)

Device physics

The drift velocity acquired by charge carriers (electrons in NMOS, holes in PMOS) per unit applied electric field: $v_d = \mu E$.

In this project: Higher electron mobility ($\mu_n \approx 3\times \mu_p$) motivated an all-NMOS pull-down switching network with resistive loads for high-frequency operation.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-mobility--mu-n--mu-p

Model Section (tt, ff, ss, fs, sf)

Verification

Named configuration blocks within SPICE model libraries defining statistical process corner variations for NMOS and PMOS transistors.

In this project: Specified in Spectre runs via `section=tt` (typical-typical), `section=ff` (fast-fast), `section=ss` (slow-slow), `section=fs` (fast-slow), and `section=sf` (slow-fast).
→ View in Section 4: Results Dashboard #term-model-section--tt--ff--ss--fs--sf

MOSFET / MOS Transistor

Device physics

Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor, a voltage-controlled switching and amplifying device utilizing an insulated gate.

In this project: The fundamental semiconductor device used throughout the frequency divider core in the Cadence GPDK045 45 nm technology kit.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-mosfet---mos-transistor

Multiphase

Circuit

An electrical clock generation architecture that produces multiple periodic output signals possessing equal frequency but systematically staggered phase angles.

In this project: The 4-latch divider produces eight distinct phases: $\phi_0 = 0^\circ, \phi_1 = 45^\circ, \phi_2 = 90^\circ, \phi_3 = 135^\circ, \phi_4 = 180^\circ, \phi_5 = 225^\circ, \phi_6 = 270^\circ, \phi_7 = 315^\circ$.
→ View in Section 1: The Paper (Kuo et al.) #term-multiphase

Multiplier (m)

Sizing

A parameter in Cadence schematic and SPICE netlists that instantiates multiple identical parallel copies of a transistor to scale total width.

In this project: Set to $m = 1$ in the unsized baseline, and $m = 4$ in Design D and Design B, effectively quadrupling transistor current drive and reducing stack resistance.
→ View in Section 2: Theory & Physics #term-multiplier--m

N

Netlist / Netlister
