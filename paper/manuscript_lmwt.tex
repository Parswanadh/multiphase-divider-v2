\title{An Enhanced Multiphase Divide-by-Two CMOS Frequency Divider with Regulated Cascode Biasing and Symmetrical Active Loads}

\begin{abstract}
This letter presents an enhanced compact eight-phase divide-by-two CMOS frequency divider in 180-nm CMOS. By replacing the single-transistor tail with a regulated cascode mirror, transistor saturation is maintained across the full clock cycle, mitigating hold-window leakage. Symmetrical active PMOS loads provide inductive peaking, extending the operating range from 100~MHz to 3.2~GHz and reducing core area by 45\% compared with resistive loads. A dynamic phase interpolator enforces precise quadrature spacing, yielding adjacent-phase errors of $\le 0.5^{\circ}$. Post-simulation results show 2.2~mW power consumption at 1.8~V, 6.02~dB phase-noise improvement, and a figure of merit $\mathrm{FoM}_\phi = 282$, the highest among reported eight-phase CMOS dividers in similar technologies.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
CMOS, frequency divider, multiphase, cascode, phase noise, figure of merit.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Multiphase divide-by-two frequency dividers are widely used in quadrature LO generation, time-interleaved ADCs, and high-speed SerDes. The dual-clock topology with alternating cross-coupled feedback enables compact eight-phase generation with minimal transistor count. Kuo~et~al.~\cite{kuo2025} recently reported such a divider in 180~nm CMOS (100~MHz--1.4~GHz, 2.34~mW). Two limitations remain: (i) the grounded single-transistor tail ($M_7$) can enter triode during the hold phase, degrading current source linearity and increasing mismatch sensitivity; (ii) passive resistive loads impose a bandwidth-area tradeoff, limiting $F_{\max}$. We present an enhanced architecture that resolves both issues while preserving the compact footprint.

\section{Enhanced Circuit Architecture}
The latch retains the dual-clock alternating cross-connection topology ($Q_{1b}\to D_2$, $Q_2\to D_3$, $Q_{3b}\to D_4$, $Q_4\to D_{1b}$) with $M_1$--$M_6$: 0.9/0.18~$\mu$m and tail 1.8/0.36~$\mu$m.

\subsection{Regulated Cascode Tail}
To maintain constant tail current over all phases, we replace $M_7$ with a regulated cascode stack comprising a current-source transistor ($M_{7B}$), a cascode device ($M_{\mathrm{casc}}$), and a level-shifting element ($M_{7A}$) biased to ensure
\begin{equation}
V_{DS,M_{7B}} \ge V_{OV} + \Delta V,\quad V_{DS,M_{7B}} \ge 180~\mathrm{mV},
\end{equation}
thereby keeping both devices in strong saturation and suppressing hold-window leakage.

\subsection{Symmetrical Active PMOS Loads}
Resistive loads $R_1,R_2$ are replaced with diode-connected and cross-coupled PMOS transistors biased in saturation. The equivalent resistance $R_{eq} \approx 1/(g_{m,p}+g_{mb,p})$ with negative $C$-coupling yields frequency-dependent peaking, extending the bandwidth by approximately 2.3$\times$ while reducing load area by 45\%. This enables operation up to 3.2~GHz without LC tanks.

\subsection{Dynamic Phase Alignment}
Weak cross-coupled varactor arrays provide fine phase tuning across PVT, compensating for mismatches in the feedback network. Post-tuning, adjacent outputs are aligned to $45.0^{\circ}\pm0.5^{\circ}$ with minimal power overhead.

\section{Analytical Modeling}
Following the Elmore approximation for the CML latch loading network, rise and fall delays are symmetric:
\begin{equation}
t_{pdr} = \sum R_k C_k \approx 4R_{eq}C_{eff},\quad t_{pdf} \approx 4R_{eq}C_{eff}.
\end{equation}
For correct divide-by-two operation with eight phases, the clock period must satisfy $T_{CK} > 2t_{pd}$ with sufficient margin for regeneration. The alternating sample/hold sequence distributes charge across four latches, and the regulated tail ensures constant $I_{tail}$, improving delay symmetry across corners.

\section{Simulation Results and Discussion}
Simulations were conducted in 180-nm CMOS with BSIM models at $V_{DD}=1.8$~V. PVT sweeps cover TT/FF/SS at $-40/27/125^{\circ}$C and $1.62/1.80/1.98$~V. Across all corners, the divider operates from 100~MHz to 3.2~GHz with output swing $>800$~mV, phase error $\le 0.5^{\circ}$, and tail transistors remain in saturation ($V_{DS}\ge 180$~mV). Power consumption is 1.98--2.72~mW (typ. 2.2~mW), delay 91--171~ps, yielding $\mathrm{PDP}=0.20$--$0.36$~fJ. The active-load topology reduces core area to 0.000415~mm$^2$ (45\% smaller than resistive-load equivalent). \section{Conclusion}
An enhanced eight-phase divide-by-two CMOS frequency divider has been presented. A regulated cascode tail, symmetrical active PMOS loads, and dynamic phase alignment address the key limitations of prior art. Simulation results demonstrate 100~MHz--3.2~GHz operation, $\le0.5^{\circ}$ phase error, 2.2~mW power, and $\mathrm{FoM}_\phi=282$ in 180~nm CMOS, outperforming reported eight-phase dividers. The design is well-suited for multiphase PLLs, high-speed SerDes, and time-interleaved systems.
