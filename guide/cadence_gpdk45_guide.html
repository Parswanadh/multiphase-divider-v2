1. Overview, deliverable map and 40-minute plan

1.1 What you are building

You are implementing the compact multiphase divide-by-two frequency divider of Kuo et al. (IEEE Microwave and Wireless Technology Letters, May 2025) in Cadence Virtuoso using the gpdk045 (45 nm) generic PDK. The divider is a ring of four cascaded differential CML-style D-latches driven by a complementary clock pair CK / CKb. The four latches are connected with alternating cross-couplings so that the ring behaves as a divide-by-two with eight equally spaced output phases at fin/2.

  • Latch cell (identical x4): sample pair M1/M2 (inputs D, Db), hold pair M3/M4 (cross-coupled), clock switches M5 (gate = CK) and M6 (gate = CKb), tail transistor M7 (NMOS in saturation, longer channel, gate on a DC bias Vbias), and load resistors R1, R2 to VDD.
  • Ring connections: Q1b to D2, Q2 to D3, Q3b to D4, Q4 to D1b. This single inversion around the ring (with the clock-phase alternation) produces the divide-by-two behaviour.
  • Outputs: eight phases named f0, f45, f90, f135, f180, f225, f270 and f315, each at fin/2 and spaced by 45 degrees of the output period, i.e. T_out/8 = T_CK/4 (since T_out = 2 T_CK). Verify this spacing in your own simulation by measuring the delay between adjacent phases.
  • Original paper values (180 nm CMOS): M1 to M6 = 0.9 um / 0.18 um, M7 = 1.8 um / 0.36 um, R1 = R2 = 1.5 kOhm, VDD = 1.8 V, fin = 1.4 GHz, total power 2.34 mW.
Scope of this guide This guide takes you from an empty directory to a simulated, characterised and (at least partially) laid-out design. Every numeric value in the specification section is a starting point. The deliverable is a design you tuned yourself with your own simulation evidence, not a copy of these numbers.

2.4 Elmore delay model

The paper estimates the latch propagation delay with a first-order Elmore model. Follow the paper's own definitions of the capacitance breakdown and cross-check them against the figure/text in the paper before quoting them in your report; the compact summary is:

  1. Let C be the unit capacitance (one transistor drain/gate capacitance contribution) of the circuit.
  2. The common node a (the node shared by the sample/hold pair above the clock switches) is treated as a small-signal ground in the differential half circuit during the transition. Its diffusion capacitance, 2C, therefore does not charge through the load and does not contribute to the output delay.
  3. The total capacitance that must be charged or discharged at the output node is 8C (diffusion of the connected devices plus gate load of the following latch and wiring, as counted in the paper).
  4. The effective resistance seen charging that capacitance is R/2, per the paper's half-circuit model.
t_pdf = t_pdr = (8C) · (R / 2) = 4RC
Td ≡ t_pd = 4RC

Since the divider is a ring of four latches clocked on alternate phases, each clock half-period must be long enough for two latch delays to propagate (the signal ripples through two latches within one half-cycle of CK). Hence the timing condition:

T_CK / 2 > 2 · Td   ⇒   fin,max < 1 / (4 Td) = 1 / (16 R C)

So the maximum input frequency is limited by two latch delays, not by one. Use this as a sanity check: after you measure Td in simulation, compute 1/(4 Td) and compare it with the highest frequency at which your simulation still divides correctly. Differences are expected (finite edge rates, nonlinear devices, clock loading) and should be discussed in the report, not hidden. Note that the formula says reducing R lowers delay but also lowers swing at fixed Itail, so R and tail current must be tuned together.

Measure C instead of guessing it After your first run you can back out an effective RC from the measured delay (C_eff = Td / (4R)) and compare it with the value you would expect from device capacitances in the DC operating point (Results > Print > DC Operating Points, look for cgs, cgd, cdb parameters). This gives you material for the 8-mark sizing-concept answer.

3.5 Finding the model file and corner sections

Spectre needs the model file of the PDK and a corner section name. Do not guess either: find them.

  1. Locate the PDK root (the directory you used in cds.lib, or ask your administrator). List its top level: 
    ls /path/to/gpdk045

    find /path/to/gpdk045 -name "*.scs" -o -name "*.lib" -o -name "*.inc" | head -50

    grep -n -i "section" /path/to/gpdk045/models/spectre/<modelfile>.scs

1.3 The library / cell / view idea

Analogy: think of a library as a folder on your disk, a cell as one design (one sub-circuit, for example DLATCH), and a view as one representation of that design, like the different file formats of a document:

  • schematic: the circuit drawing (this is what you build).
  • symbol: the box with pins you drop into other schematics.
  • layout: the physical mask drawing (not needed for this task).
  • config: tells the tools which view of each sub-cell to use when netlisting (mostly for mixed hierarchies; you can ignore it).
  • av_extracted: the extracted post-layout view with parasitics (only after layout and extraction).

So "the latch's schematic" is library divider_lib, cell DLATCH, view schematic. The PDK itself is another library (gpdk045) that contains cells such as the NMOS transistor, each with a symbol view. When you place a device you are placing "library / cell / view" of the PDK.

Why Check and Save matters Closing a window or just pressing the regular Save (File > Save) stores the drawing, but only Check and Save (Design > Check and Save) verifies connectivity and also updates the internal connectivity data that the netlister uses. A schematic that was drawn but not checked and saved may netlist an old version, or fail to netlist at all. Make it a reflex: after every few edits, Check and Save, then read the CIW.

Tutorial: build ONE latch schematic from nothing

about 15 min Goal: a cell DLATCH in a new library divider_lib, with 7 NMOS transistors M1..M7, 2 load resistors, 9 pins, and a symbol. You will then reuse it four times.

Circuit in one paragraph This is a differential (current-mode-logic style) D-latch. R1 and R2 pull the outputs Q and Qb up to VDD. The sample pair M1/M2 (gates D and Db) steers current while the clock switch M5 (gate CK) is on; the hold pair M3/M4 (cross-coupled: gate of M3 is Qb, gate of M4 is Q) keeps the state while the opposite switch M6 (gate CKb) is on. M7 is the tail current source, biased by NBIAS. The cross-coupled pair needs loop gain gm*R greater than 1 to regenerate, so the load resistor must not be too small.

2.1 Create the library

  1. In the CIW choose File > New > Library (verify in your version; in the Library Manager the same entry is under its own File menu).
  2. In the "New Library" form type Name divider_lib. Leave the directory as proposed (your project directory) so the library is created next to cds.lib. Click OK/Apply.
  3. A second form asks about technology: choose Attach to an existing technology library and click OK.
  4. In the "Attach Design Library to Technology Library" form, set the Technology Library drop-down to gpdk045. The exact name depends on your PDK installation (it could be gpdk045 or a variant): verify the name in your list. If the list is empty or has no 45 nm entry, your cds.lib does not include the PDK; ask your lab support (the PDK must be defined in cds.lib via an INCLUDE or DEFINE line).
  5. Click OK. Read the CIW: a line about the new library and the tech attach should be shown, with no error.
  6. Open the Library Manager (Tools > Library Manager). Click divider_lib in the left column. It is still empty.

2.2 Create the DLATCH cell

  1. With divider_lib selected, choose File > New > Cell View (the Library Manager menu; verify in your version).
  2. Library: divider_lib. Cell: DLATCH (all capitals, to match this guide). View: schematic. Type: set to schematic (the Tool field is often filled automatically as "Composer-Schematic" or "Schematic L/XL"; accept the default).
  3. Click OK. An empty Virtuoso Schematic Editor window opens. Maximise it. Click once inside the drawing area to give it focus.
  4. Immediately press Shift+X to Check and Save once, so that the cell exists on disk.

2.3 Place the seven transistors M1..M7

Click the symbol view. The form fields Library / Cell / View fill in. Close the browser.
  3. The form now shows the instance parameters of the device (typically width, length, number of fingers; the exact names come from the PDK's CDF, so read them in the form; verify). Type the starting values: W = 240n, L = 45n. Leave fingers at 1.
  4. Move the mouse to the drawing area. A ghost of the transistor follows the cursor. Press r to rotate if you wish. Click once to drop one device.
  5. Click six more times to drop six more copies (the command stays active). Press Esc when done. You now have seven NMOS instances, probably named M0, M1, ... or I0, I1 ... automatically.

2.4 Place the two load resistors

  1. Press i, click Browse, library analogLib, cell res, view symbol. Set Resistance to 8k (start value, to be tuned). Place two copies and name them R1 and R2 with q.
  2. analogLib res is an ideal resistor and is acceptable for a quick start. gpdk045 also provides its own resistor cells (poly or well resistors, names vary): verify in your PDK's library browser and switch to one of them before layout. An ideal resistor has no parasitic capacitance and no layout, so it cannot be used in layout-versus-schematic.
Why the load is 8k and not 1.5k In a generic-model pre-check (explicitly not gpdk045) a load of 1.5 kOhm was too small: the cross-coupled pair needs loop gain gm*R greater than 1 to latch and hold, and with the small resistor the latch did not regenerate. 8 kOhm is therefore the starting value here. Other documents in this project may say 1.5k; treat the number as a tunable parameter, check the DC drop (tail current times R must leave the outputs enough above ground to keep M1..M6 in saturation), and re-tune with gpdk045 models.

2.7 Visual checks before saving

  1. Press f to fit the view, then zoom in on each device and look at its four terminals. Every terminal needs a wire ending exactly on it (no gap).
  2. Count the net names: you should see exactly Q, Qb, xs, xh, tail, D, Db, CK, CKb, NBIAS, VDD, VSS. Any other name (for example automatically generated net1) means an unlabelled or accidentally separate segment.
  3. Click each net name on the drawing: the whole net highlights. If only a piece highlights, your wire is broken into separate nets.
  4. Reconfirm all W and L values by selecting the instance and pressing q. Reconfirm the bulk pins go to VSS.

2.8 Check and Save and how to read it

  1. Press Shift+X, or choose Design > Check and Save.
  2. Look at the CIW (and, if the schematic window shows one, the status line at the bottom). The result is something like "Extracting connectivity ... Schematic check completed with 0 errors and 0 warnings" (exact wording differs by version).
  3. Errors (red markers in the schematic): must be fixed. Typical ones: duplicate instance name, shorted wires with two different labels, a pin bound to a different net.
  4. Warnings (yellow markers): read each one. Meaning of the common ones:
    • "Dangling wire" or "floating wire": a wire end connected to nothing; delete the stub or connect it.
    • "Net has only one connection" / "floating net": a label that connects to nothing else; probably a typo in a net name.
    • "Pin not connected": a pin with no wire.
    • "Two names for one net": two labels on the same wire; keep one.
    Warnings do not always block simulation, but warnings you do not understand are usually real mistakes.
  5. Use Check > Find Marker (verify in your version) to jump to a marker, or click a marker to see its message.

2.9 Create the symbol

  1. In the DLATCH schematic window choose Create > Cellview > From Cellview (verify in your version).
  2. The "Cellview From Cellview" form shows From View schematic, To View symbol and Tool/Type "SchematicSymbol". Click OK.
  3. The "Symbol Generation Options" form appears. Position the pins: the left side usually receives inputs (D, Db, CK, CKb, NBIAS), the right side outputs (Q, Qb), the top VDD, the bottom VSS. Click OK.
  4. The Symbol Editor opens, showing a box with pin stubs. Drag pins and the box outline only if you wish; leave pin names alone. A neat symbol saves time later when you wire four instances.
  5. Tidy it: you may enlarge the box a little so labels do not overlap; do not delete the selection-box or the instance-label placeholders (they carry the instance name).
  6. Check and Save the symbol with Shift+X (in the Symbol Editor too; verify in your version), then close the symbol window. In the Library Manager you should now see under divider_lib / DLATCH two views: schematic and symbol.
If you change the pin list later Adding or removing a pin in the schematic does not update the symbol automatically. Re-run Create > Cellview > From Cellview and let it update the symbol, then check that any higher-level instances still connect correctly.

2.11 divider_core pin-by-pin table

Interconnect rule: the data pair of each latch comes from the previous latch's outputs. Q1b goes to D2; Q2 goes to D3; Q3b goes to D4; Q4 goes to D1b (that is, latch 1 receives D = Q4b, Db = Q4). Latches 2 and 3 receive swapped clocks (CK pin to the net ckb, CKb pin to the net ck); latches 1 and 4 receive the normal clocks.

The core's own pins: inputs ck ckb nbias, outputs Q1 Q1b Q2 Q2b Q3 Q3b Q4 Q4b, inputOutput vdd VSS. Net names in the table that look lower-case (vdd, ck) are the names used in the reference Spectre netlist; keep your capitalisation consistent with whatever you use at the testbench. In the testbench the ground reference must be the global node 0 (placed with a gnd symbol), so that the core's VSS pin is wired to it. Run Check and Save, then create the symbol view for divider_core exactly as in 2.9.

Why the swap? Latches 2 and 3 are clocked with the opposite phase from 1 and 4, so that data passes through alternately and the ring behaves like a master/slave chain with the inverted feedback from Q4 to D1b. If you forget the swap, or if one inversion is missing, the ring will not toggle. If the circuit does not divide later, re-read this table before anything else.

    Initial V1.001.0001.001.0
  4. Click OK. The values are written to the netlist as ic statements. If your ADE only offers "Nodeset" (Convergence Aids > Nodeset), nodeset is a hint to the DC solver and is less strict than ic; for a transient start ic is what you want. Verify in your version.

3.12 Waveform viewer basics

  • Zoom: in the toolbar choose the zoom tool and drag a box in the plot area; the f key fits all data in the window (verify in your version). The View menu offers zoom in/out and reset.
  • Markers: Marker > Create Marker (verify), click on a waveform to place a marker. Place two markers and use the "Delta" (a "Create Delta Marker" or dx readout) to read a time difference: the period of CK and the period of Q1.
  • Strips: by default all curves are in one plot. Use Axis > Strip > Add or the "Split Current Strip" / "Add Strip" toolbar buttons (verify), then drag a curve into the new strip to stack the clock above the outputs. Stacked strips share the same time axis.

  • Calculator: open Tools > Calculator (verify). Click vt (voltage versus time) and then on a net in the schematic; the expression is built in the buffer. Click - after a second net, to build Q1 minus Q1b, then Plot.
  • Delete / hide: select a curve, press Delete, or toggle its visibility in the legend.

3.13 How to tell "it is dividing"

f(Q1) = f(CK) / 2, equivalently period(Q1) = 2 x period(CK)
  1. Plot ck (top strip) and Q1 (second strip) over the same time range.
  2. Put marker A on one rising edge of ck and marker B on the next rising edge: the delta is the clock period (1 ns for a 1 GHz clock).
  3. Put marker A on one rising edge of Q1 and marker B on the next rising edge of Q1. If the delta is twice the clock period (2 ns), the circuit divides by 2.
  4. Also confirm that Q1 and Q1b are complementary and swing by a reasonable amount (do not trust a plot where the swing is a few millivolts; in that case the latch is not regenerating, so re-check R and bias).
  5. The 8 phases: Q1, Q1b, Q2, Q2b, Q3, Q3b, Q4, Q4b should be evenly spaced in time within one output period. Placing delta markers between corresponding edges gives the phase spacing, ideally one eighth of the output period. I give no target numbers here because none were measured on gpdk045; the measured spacing is your result.
  6. If instead the outputs are static, or toggle at the same rate as the clock, or ring at a strange frequency, return to the cheatsheet in the Cadence from zero section and also try (a) a lower fin (500 MHz), (b) larger load R, (c) higher or lower VB, (d) tight accuracy and maximum step.
Starting values, not results VDD = 1.0 V, M1..M6 at 240n/45n, M7 at 480n/90n, load R about 8k, VB about 0.8 V, 10 fF per output, fin = 1 GHz, clock edges 20 ps. These come from a pre-check that was NOT run with gpdk045 models. Expect to tune the bias, the load and the sizes. Do not quote them as findings.

4.3 If it does not divide: a short debug order

  1. Is the swap right? XD2 and XD3 must have CK and CKb swapped; XD4's Q goes to XD1's Db.
  2. Is the initial condition applied? Check the netlist for ic.
  3. Does one latch alone latch? Temporarily simulate a single DLATCH with static D and a clock to check that the output follows D when CK is high and holds when CKb is high. If the output swing is tiny, loop gain gm*R is too small (raise R, W of M3/M4, or the bias current via VB); if the swing is huge but slow, reduce R or C.
  4. Is the tail current sensible? Look at the DC current of the VDD source (VDD:p in the standalone netlist) and the DC voltage of node tail; M7 must be in saturation (its drain-source voltage above its overdrive). VB at about 0.8 V is only a starting guess.
  5. Try a lower fin first (for example 500 MHz or 250 MHz) and raise it once division works.
  6. If everything fails, report exactly which of the above you checked and what you saw; a clearly documented failure is more useful than an invented success.

What the pre-check simulation showed

Before writing this guide I ran the topology in ngspice with generic LEVEL=1 models (spice/divider_45nm_proxy.cir). This is a topology and startup check only. It is not gpdk045, none of its numbers belong in your report, and the sizes below will move on the real BSIM models. What carries over is the behaviour, because it comes from the circuit and not from the model card.

Read this before tuning in Cadence The three behaviours below each cost time if you meet them cold. All three were observed in the proxy runs listed here. Expect the same shapes in Spectre, at different values.

1. The load must give the hold pair gain above 1

With the original-ratio loads (R = 1.5 kΩ, M1–M6 = 240n/45n) the loop never started dividing at any tail bias from 0.7 to 1.0 V (R = 1.5k and 3k). Inspecting one run (Vbias = 1.0 V) showed Q and Qb of each latch sitting at the same voltage, about 0.92 V. The cross-coupled pair needs gm·R > 1 to regenerate, and my hand estimate for these devices was just under 1 (an estimate, not a measurement). Raising R fixed it:

Takeaway for Spectre: if the outputs sit at one voltage and never separate, increase R or the tail current before suspecting the wiring. In the schematic, check the DC operating point of Q and Qb of one latch.

2. Phase spacing depends on latch delay, not only on the clock

The outputs came out as four pairs, not eight evenly spaced edges. The adjacent steps alternate between a and 90° − a with a = 360 · Td · fin. Measured at R = 8k, Vbias = 0.8 V, CL = 5 fF:

finMeasured step pair (deg)Implied Td
1 GHz86.0 / 4.0about 11 ps
2 GHz82.0 / 8.0about 11 ps

a = 360° · Td · fin   ⇒   uniform 45° steps need   TCK = 8 · Td

This is exactly the "TCK ≈ 8Td" condition from the paper's discussion, and it ties into the sizing marks. The 8 phases are equally spaced only at the operating point where the latch delay is one eighth of the clock period. At 180 nm with Td near 90 ps and fin = 1.4 GHz that point sits inside the paper's range. A 45 nm latch is much faster, so at the same frequency the steps bunch up.

I tried to slow the proxy latch with output capacitance to reach 45°. At 2 GHz and CL = 14 fF the pair moved to 68.1 / 21.9, but 10 fF at 2 GHz failed to divide (ratio 0.99), and CL of 20 fF or more failed to divide cleanly at 2–3 GHz. The window between "too fast, uneven" and "too slow, no division" is narrow, and I did not reach uniform 45° in the proxy. Treat this as the main design question to answer in gpdk045: pick fin, then tune R, load and tail current until the measured steps approach 45°, and report the phase error you actually get.

Use this in the report Plot measured phase step versus fin from your own sweep and overlay 360·Td·fin using your measured Td. A curve that follows the formula is strong evidence for the sizing and delay sections.

Wire naming, bulk connections and LVS

  • Name every net in both the cell and the parent. Spectre netlists use these names, so your waveform viewer shows /Q1 rather than net012, and the OCEAN/Calculator expressions in the next sections stay stable between edits.
  • Bulk connections: connect every NMOS bulk to VSS explicitly with a wire (not by a global such as gnd!). Implicit bulks lead to a floating or wrongly tied well and to LVS mismatches at the substrate.
  • Pins as the LVS interface: LVS compares the pin names of the schematic with the text labels (pin shapes) in the layout. Names such as VDD, VSS, CK, CKb, Q1 must be written identically (case sensitive) on a metal label in the layout. A mismatch shows up as "pin not found" or as a merged net.
  • Dummy devices: if you later add dummy gates or dummy fingers for matching or density, either also place them in the schematic (gate, source and drain tied to VSS) or confirm that your LVS deck ignores them. Decide this now; retrofitting a schematic after layout is slow.
  • Do not use the same name for two things: an internal node called Q in DLATCH is local; at top level the hierarchy separates it. Never connect two different pins with the same label by accident (for example CK and CKb typed with different case on a net).

5. Initial conditions: why and how

This divider is a regenerative ring. With all nodes starting from the DC operating point and with a perfectly symmetric netlist, Spectre can find a symmetric, metastable solution (Q equal to Qb in every latch) and stay there: the clock toggles but the outputs never split. Real circuits start because of noise; a noise-free simulation does not. Two remedies exist:

  1. Initial conditions (ic), preferred. They force the node voltages at time zero. In ADE: Simulation > Convergence Aids > Initial Condition (some versions use Setup > Initial Condition; verify in your version) and add the nodes listed below. The equivalent Spectre statement is 
    ic Q1=1.0 Q1b=0.7 Q2=1.0 Q2b=0.7 Q3=0.7 Q3b=1.0 Q4=0.7 Q4b=1.0
  2. Node set (nodeset), only a hint for the DC solver (Simulation > Convergence Aids > Node Set, verify). It helps convergence but does not remove the metastable solution if that is also a valid DC point, so use ic for forcing.

The logic values in the task statement (Q1 = 1, Q2 = 1, Q3 = 0, Q4 = 0, each with its complement inverted) map to voltages as follows. A CML-style latch is not rail to rail: the high level is VDD, the low level is VDD minus the swing (approximately Iss times R). Use your own DC value after the first run.

Measurements in ADE and OCEAN

about 60 min All expressions below are entered in the ADE Explorer/Assembler output table (Outputs > Setup, then Open Calculator and Send To the table, or type directly into the Expression column). Use differential voltages for timing: the low level of a CML latch is not 0 V, so a fixed threshold such as 0.5 V may never be crossed. The differential zero crossing is independent of the common-mode level.

Window times Let T = 1/fin = 1 ns and Tout = 2T = 2 ns at 1 GHz. With a stop time of 40 T = 40 ns, measure between t1 = 20 T = 20 ns and t2 = 40 T = 40 ns. This window contains exactly ten output periods, which matters for averages. The numbers change when fin changes; define t1 and t2 as variables.

1. Output frequency

Define the differential signal first (or use VT("/Q1") - VT("/Q1b") inline), for example output name dQ1:

dQ1 = VT("/Q1") - VT("/Q1b")

;; period from two consecutive rising zero crossings (late in the run)
Tmeas = cross(dQ1 0 12 "rising") - cross(dQ1 0 11 "rising")
fout  = 1 / Tmeas
ratio = fout / fin                  ; expect 0.500

The Calculator also has frequency(wave) (verify in your version), which can be applied to clip(dQ1 t1 t2). A cross-based average is more robust: fout_avg = (N-1) / (cross(dQ1 0 N "rising") - cross(dQ1 0 1 "rising")) for a window clipped to the settled part. For a sanity check also measure the clock: fclk = 1 / (cross(VT("/CK") - VT("/CKb") 0 12 "rising") - cross(VT("/CK") - VT("/CKb") 0 11 "rising")).

2. Eight-phase delays and phase error

Take Q1 as the reference. For each stage k define dQk likewise and find the first crossing inside one output period after the reference edge:

tref = cross(dQ1 0 12 "rising")
t_k  = cross(clip(dQk tref (tref + Tout)) 0 1 "rising") - tref      ; k = 2, 3, 4
t_kf = cross(clip(dQk tref (tref + Tout)) 0 1 "falling") - tref     ; complement phases

The ideal phases are multiples of 45 degrees. With Tout = 2/fin one phase step is Tout/8 in time. The phase error of phase index k (k = 0 for Q1, 1 for Q2, 2 for Q3, 3 for Q4; k + 4 for the complement edges):

phase_err(k) [deg] = ( t_meas(k) - k * Tout / 8 ) / Tout * 360

In the calculator this is for example phErr2 = (t_2 - 1*Tout/8)/Tout*360. Expected nominal table:

3. Average power

The supply source V0 delivers current; Spectre defines the current into the positive terminal, so it is negative when the source supplies power. The average power over an integer number of output periods is:

P = average(clip(-i("/V0/PLUS" ?result "tran") * vdd  t1 t2))
;; equivalent: vdd * average(clip(-IT("/V0/PLUS") t1 t2))

Notes: (a) make sure t2 - t1 is a multiple of Tout (here ten output periods), otherwise the average has a ripple error of a few percent; (b) this is the core power only: the clock sources are ideal and not counted, and the bias source delivers almost no current; (c) quote the load capacitance used.

4. CK to Q delay

Use 50 % to 50 % thresholds. The clock threshold is vdd/2 (or the zero crossing of CK - CKb); the output threshold is the differential zero or the mid-swing level. A robust formulation is the difference of two crossings:

tCK = cross(VT("/CK") - VT("/CKb") 0 25 "rising")
tQ  = cross(clip(dQ1 tCK (tCK + T)) 0 1 "either")
Td  = tQ - tCK

The Calculator delay() form is (verify argument names in your version):

Td = delay(?wf1 VT("/CK") ?value1 vdd/2 ?edge1 "rising" ?nth1 25
           ?wf2 dQ1       ?value2 0       ?edge2 "either" ?nth2 1
           ?td2 tCK)

Which clock edge triggers the Q edge depends on the latch phase; the "either" edge picks the first output transition after the clock edge, and you should check by plotting once. If the delay is negative or larger than T, the search window is wrong.

5. Power-delay product with units

PDP = P x Td

Example: P = 2.34 mW and Td = 120 ps give PDP = 2.34 mW x 120 ps = 280.8 fJ. Since 1 mW x 1 ps = 10-3 W x 10-12 s = 10-15 J = 1 fJ, a number of mW multiplied by a number of ps gives fJ directly. (The numbers here only show the arithmetic; your own results will differ.) In the Calculator, work in SI units and convert at the end: PDP_fJ = P * Td * 1e15. Energy per divided cycle (P/fin) is a useful second figure.

6. fmax search

  1. Create a parametric sweep of fin. In ADE Explorer: Tools > Parametric Analysis (verify), variable fin, values such as 0.5G 1G 2G 3G 4G 5G 6G (start low and raise it; the maximum frequency in gpdk045 will differ from any generic-model estimate). In ADE Assembler: type the list in the Global Variables sweep field (1G 2G 3G ...) or add a parameter set.
  2. Make everything else scale with fin: T = 1/fin, stop time 40/fin, maxstep = T/100, tr = min(20p, T/10), and the measurement window t1 = 20 T, t2 = 40 T.
  3. Define "still divides" in terms of your measurements:
    • fout/fin within 0.5 +/- 1 % in the whole window (no skipped or extra transitions).
    • Differential output swing at least 50 % of its low-frequency value (or at least 100 mV peak differential).
    • Maximum phase error below a limit you state, for example 10 degrees (a second, looser limit for "divides but poor phases" is useful).
    • Outputs stay periodic over the last ten output periods (no slow amplitude decay).
  4. fmax is the highest fin that meets all criteria. Refine by bisection between the last good and the first failing frequency (e.g., 7 GHz and 8 GHz, then 7.5 GHz). Repeat for the minimum frequency; very low fin may fail because of the leaky or too slow hold phase, or may cause self-oscillation (verify).

8. Sanity checks

  • Output frequency is fin/2 (ratio 0.500) and stays so for the whole window.
  • Each output swings between a low level and VDD with a differential peak of the order of 0.3 V (not 0 mV and not a full 1.0 V swing). A rail-to-rail swing means the resistive load is too large or the tail current too high.
  • Eight phases spaced by 45 degrees (Tout/8 apart); the sum of the eight consecutive spacings is 360 degrees.
  • Q and Qb are complementary and cross each other at the same instant (common mode stays near the mid-level).
  • Supply current is a repetitive wave with dc average of about P / VDD. A current of several mA at 45 nm points to a bias or biasing mistake.
  • Delay Td is smaller than a quarter of T (250 ps at 1 GHz, about 83 ps at 3 GHz); otherwise the latch is too slow for the target.
  • Results converge when errpreset is changed from moderate to conservative and when maxstep is halved; if not, the measurement is dominated by numerical error.

Layout in Virtuoso (gpdk045)

The layout is worth 5 of the 40 lab marks, but it also feeds the PVT/post-layout story, so build it carefully and keep it simple. The goal is a compact, symmetric, DRC-clean, LVS-clean four-latch core that you can extract and re-simulate. This section follows the layout decisions described for the paper (two-finger unit nMOS, M7 adjacent to the clock block, symmetric clock routing) but every dimension below is your own to choose and measure.

Menu names varyCadence menu paths differ between IC6.1.x and IC23.x and between Layout XL, GXL and the Layout Suite. Anything written as Menu > Item below is the common form; verify in your version. Layer names and rule decks are PDK specific, so look them up in your own tech file and do not copy layer names from another PDK.

1. Prepare the schematic before touching layout

  • Run Check and Save on the latch schematic and on the top-level divider schematic with zero errors and zero warnings you cannot explain.
  • Decide the finger and multiplier policy now. For the paper-style unit device, set each nMOS to fingers = 2 and halve the per-finger width so that total width is unchanged (for example, W_total = 240n becomes 2 fingers of 120n each). Record the choice in your report.
  • Make sure the schematic has real pins: CK, CKb, VDD, VSS, nbias (if you bias M7 from a pin), and the output pins for all four latches (8 outputs: Q and Qb for each of the four phases).
  • Resistor loads R1 and R2 should be the PDK resistor cell you will actually use in layout (verify in your PDK which resistor type matches your target sheet resistance and whether it has a model for the library you simulate with). A mismatched resistor view is the most common reason LVS fails late.

2. Generate from source

  1. Open the latch (or divider) schematic, then Launch > Layout XL (or Layout GXL). Choose Create New and accept the layout view name layout.
  2. In Layout XL choose Connectivity > Generate > All From Source. In the dialog confirm that pins, instances and IO pins are all selected. Set a reasonable pin layer and pin label option. Verify in your version whether pin type and layer are picked from the schematic or set here.
  3. Click OK. All devices appear stacked or scattered in the layout window with rubber-band connectivity (airwires). If devices are missing, the schematic was not saved or the source library is wrong.
  4. Use Connectivity > Update > Components and Nets every time you change the schematic after this point, so the layout stays tied to the source. Do not edit the schematic and layout independently.
  5. Turn on Connectivity > Check > Against Source (verify in your version) and keep the Design Checks / connectivity panel open to see shorts and opens while routing.

3. Floorplan and placement strategy

Build and verify one latch first, then instantiate it four times. A hierarchical divider (latch layout cell placed four times) is much easier to get symmetric than one flat sea of devices, and LVS is faster to debug.

5. Routing and metal usage

  • Layer names: check the layer list in your tech file (Layer Selection Window, LSW) for the poly, contact, metal1, via1, metal2, and so on. Do not assume names from other PDKs.
  • Local connections inside a latch (drain/source straps, gate connections) on the lowest metal. Use the next metal up for vertical signal tracks and the upper metals for clock and power trunks.
  • Clock (CK, CKb): route as a symmetric pair with the same width, same layers, same number of vias, and same length to every clock switch. Keep them away from the output nodes and from each other only as far as needed; unequal coupling to Q/Qb contributes to phase error.
  • Output nodes (Q, Qb of each latch): keep short and balanced; they carry the dominant capacitance in the delay model, so every extra wire length costs speed and adds mismatch.
  • Power: wide VDD and VSS straps with multiple vias. IR drop is small at this current level, but narrow rails do matter for resistor load connections.
  • Vias: use via arrays where the width allows; keep both branches of a differential pair identical.
  • Do not run a long signal wire over the clock switches or tail node; stray capacitance on the tail node slows transitions.

6. Pins and labels

  1. Create pins with Create > Pin (or use pins generated by Generate from Source) on a metal layer that has a pin purpose in your tech file.
  2. Pin names must exactly match schematic pin names (case sensitive). Eight output pins (for example Q1, Q1b, Q2, Q2b, Q3, Q3b, Q4, Q4b, or whatever names your schematic uses), plus CK, CKb, VDD, VSS, and nbias if used.
  3. Place CK/CKb on one edge (symmetric), outputs on the opposite or adjacent edges in phase order, and VDD/VSS on the outer ring so that a testbench can connect cleanly.
  4. Attach text labels on the same layer as the metal (many LVS decks need label text on a text layer matching the metal). Verify the label/text layer rule for your PDK; this is the source of the "pin text mismatch" error in the table in the next section.

Part B. Sizing concept (8 marks)

B1. Equal rise and fall argument

The differential output nodes should have matching rise and fall times so that the output is symmetric and the phases are evenly spaced. In a resistor-loaded current-steering stage:

  • Following Kuo et al. (Sec. II and Fig. 5): the output falls through the ON transistor path and rises through the load resistor R1.
  • The paper designs the nMOS turn-on resistance R to be twice the load resistance R1, i.e. R1 = R/2. M7 is always on, so node b is treated as ground.
  • With that choice the falling and rising transitions see the same effective resistance (R/2) and the same lumped capacitance, so the rise and fall times match (tpdr = tpdf). Cite the paper for this sizing rule and state that it is a first-order argument.
  • State that this is a first-order, hand-calculation argument; the tuned values come from simulation, and it is acceptable that they differ from hand values if you explain why.

B2. Tail device M7

  • Use a longer channel for M7 (starting point L = 90n versus 45n for the others) so that it behaves like a current source in saturation, with higher output resistance (less channel-length modulation), and so that its current changes little with the voltage on the tail node.
  • M7 must stay in saturation: V_DS(M7) must exceed V_dsat = V_GS - V_t. Write the headroom inequality: VDD - I_tail*R - V_DS(M1,M5 stack) must leave room for V_DS(M7) > V_dsat. If the tail is pushed into triode, the current drops and the speed and swing fall.
  • The swing is set by I_tail*R. Specify the target swing as ___ mV and show the resulting I_tail = swing / R.

B3. Elmore delay derivation, step by step

The Elmore delay of an RC network from a source to node n is the sum, over every capacitor k, of C_k multiplied by the resistance that is common to the path from the source to node k and the path from the source to node n:

t_D(n) = Σk Ck · Rnk
  1. Draw the RC model of the D-latch for the falling output, as in the paper's Fig. 5(a)-(b): assume the gate capacitance C equals the diffusion capacitance of one unit; node a has already discharged to ground through capacitance 2C and resistance R/2.
  2. Count the capacitance in units of C, as the paper does: node Q1 carries (6+2)C = 8C. Show your own count of which devices contribute how many units (fill in the table below). If your count differs from the paper's, show it and derive with your count.
  3. Take the resistance in the discharge path: the paper gives R/2 from node Q1 to ground (from the design choice R1 = R/2 above). Reproduce Fig. 5(b) in your own drawing.
  4. Apply the Elmore formula:
    tpdf = (8C) · (R/2) = 4RC
  5. Repeat for the rising edge. By the symmetry of the differential pair and the equal-resistance sizing, the rising transition sees the same effective resistance and capacitance, so
    tpdr = (8C) · (R/2) = 4RC = tpdf
  6. Conclude: equal rise and fall delays follow from R = 2*R1, and the delay scales with R and C; reducing parasitic capacitance (for example by two-finger layout and sharing diffusion) reduces delay directly.
  7. Convert 0.69 factor if asked: the Elmore delay is the first moment; the 50 percent propagation delay of a single-pole RC is about 0.69 RC. Say which you are reporting.

Viva: what examiners probably ask

  1. Why does the circuit divide by two? Latches clocked on opposite phases form a loop with an inversion; the state flips once per half input period, so the output toggles at half the input frequency.
  2. Why four latches rather than two? Four latches give multiple equally spaced phase outputs from one divider (eight outputs with complements); two latches give only quadrature. State the phases you observe.
  3. What does M7 do? It sets the tail current; it is longer so it behaves as a current source in saturation.
  4. What does R = 2*R1 achieve? It balances the charging and discharging resistances, so t_pdr = t_pdf.
  5. What limits maximum frequency? The RC time constant at the output nodes (about 4RC to first order) and the clock-switch drive; below the lower limit the circuit may fail to regenerate, above the upper limit it stops dividing correctly.
  6. Why two fingers? Shared diffusion lowers drain/source junction capacitance, so smaller C, faster nodes.
  7. Why symmetric clock routing? Unequal wire length gives unequal delay and skews the phases, increasing phase error.
  8. What if the supply drops? Smaller overdrive means higher on-resistance and lower fmax; headroom for M7 shrinks. Support your answer with your PVT run.
  9. How did you pick R? From the swing target and the tail current (R = swing / I_tail), then checked R against 2*R1; tuned by simulation.
  10. What changes after layout? Extra parasitic capacitance and resistance on the nodes lowers fmax and shifts phase; show your own pre versus post-layout comparison.
